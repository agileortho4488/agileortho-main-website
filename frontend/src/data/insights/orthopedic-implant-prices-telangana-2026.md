These are supply ranges for hospital billing in Telangana; patient-side MRP billing under insurance schemes follows the hospital's own rate cards.

## The five things that change your quote

1. **Volume commitment.** A hospital doing 20 trauma cases a month gets a different rate card than one doing 2. Annual rate contracts beat case-by-case buying.
2. **Payment cycle.** 30-day payers get sharper prices than 120-day payers — credit cost is real and it is priced in.
3. **Loaner sets vs consignment.** If the distributor's instrumentation set travels free with each case, that service is part of the price. Hospitals with purchased in-house sets negotiate lower per-implant rates.
4. **Brand tier.** Indian premium (Meril, and comparable) sits well below imported multinationals with comparable clinical performance for standard trauma — that gap is 30 to 60%.
5. **Emergency service.** Same-night dispatch capability costs the distributor standing inventory; partners who need it should expect it priced into the relationship, not each invoice.

## Why the cheapest quote is often the expensive one

A low per-piece price with incomplete size runs means intra-op scrambling and cancelled lists. Judge a supplier on: complete size-run availability, batch traceability on every challan, set turnaround time, and how they behave when a case goes to midnight. The invoice is only part of the cost of an implant partner.
