Until recently, buying orthopedic implants meant phone calls, WhatsApp photos of rate lists and a rep visit. In 2026, hospitals, nursing homes and sub-dealers increasingly order online — but implants are not office supplies. Here is what a safe online implant purchase looks like, and how to use it to your advantage on price and speed.

## Who should buy implants online

- **Nursing homes and smaller hospitals** without daily rep coverage — online ordering gets metro rate transparency in district towns.
- **Sub-dealers and surgical stores** replenishing fast movers (screws, plates, sutures) between bulk orders.
- **Purchase departments** that want an auditable trail — every online order leaves a record that phone orders never do.

Complex case-specific sets (a full nailing system for tomorrow's list) still deserve a call — good online stores show a phone number precisely for that.

## The six checks before you order from any implant website

1. **Licence:** the seller must hold a wholesale drug licence covering medical devices; implants are regulated as Class B/C devices under CDSCO rules.
2. **Batch and sterility traceability:** every implant should ship with batch number, sterility validity and manufacturer certificate — if the site can't promise that on the invoice, walk away.
3. **Real brand authorization:** authorized distributors (we are authorized for Meril, among others) get you genuine product and warranty support; grey-market pricing usually hides re-sterilized or near-expiry stock.
4. **Backorder handling:** a serious supplier lets you order even when stock shows zero — they procure against your order and commit a date. No-stock-no-order stores are catalogs, not suppliers.
5. **GST invoicing** in your institution's name — required for insurance-scheme billing and audits.
6. **Returns policy for unopened sterile packs** — case cancellations happen; know the policy before, not after.

## What online ordering does to your prices

Online rate cards remove the information gap between metro and district buyers. Volume discounts that were rep-negotiated become visible tiers, and coupon-driven launch pricing (common in 2026 as distributors move online) can put transacted prices 20–30% below traditional quotes. Combine that with backordering and a district hospital can run leaner inventory than was ever possible on phone-and-rep supply.
