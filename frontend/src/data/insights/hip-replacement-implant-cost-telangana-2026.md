Hip replacement purchasing decisions get made twice: once by the surgeon (which system, which fixation) and once by procurement (what it costs, how fast it arrives). Here is what both sides need in 2026.

## Fixation choice drives price first

- **Cemented stems** — lower implant cost, faster primary fixation, preferred for older or lower-bone-quality patients.
- **Uncemented (press-fit) stems** — higher cost, porous-coated for biological ingrowth, preferred for younger, active patients with good bone stock.
- **Dual mobility cups** — an added cost line, but reduce dislocation risk meaningfully in revision-risk patients (prior surgery, neuromuscular conditions).

A district hospital doing routine primary THR in older patients will usually specify cemented; a metro center doing younger active patients leans uncemented — the same "hip replacement" quote can vary 30–40% purely on this choice.

## What actually moves the price on a quote

1. **Head material** — ceramic heads cost more than cobalt-chrome but reduce wear debris over the implant's life; relevant for younger patients expecting 20+ years of use.
2. **Bundle vs à la carte** — cup, liner, stem, and head priced as a system bundle is usually cheaper than ordering each component separately.
3. **Loaner instrumentation** — whether the surgical instrument set travels with the implant at no extra charge, or is billed/rented separately.
4. **Volume and payment terms** — same as trauma implants, hospitals on standing purchase agreements get materially better pricing than one-off case orders.
