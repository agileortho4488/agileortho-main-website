Diagnostic labs switching ELISA reagent suppliers care about exactly three things: does the kit perform consistently batch to batch, does the cold-chain hold on delivery, and can the supplier actually restock before you run out. Here's what matters when evaluating MeriSera and MeriLisa for your lab.

## What the two kit families cover

**MeriSera** and **MeriLisa** are Meril's ELISA reagent kit lines used across serology and immunoassay testing in hospital pathology labs and standalone diagnostic centers. Exact panel coverage (which analytes/markers a given kit tests for) varies by SKU — labs should confirm the specific kit code against their testing menu before switching, since panel coverage is not identical across the range.

## Cold-chain: the real switching risk

ELISA reagents are temperature-sensitive. The two failure modes labs actually see when switching suppliers:

1. **Transit excursion** — reagent leaves 2–8°C during transport and degrades silently; the kit still runs but gives unreliable results.
2. **Storage lapse** — reagent sits correctly delivered but improperly stored at the lab before use.

Ask any new supplier for their cold-chain validation on the specific delivery route to your facility, not a generic claim. ## Batch consistency and validation

Before switching a lab's routine testing menu to a new kit, run a parallel validation batch — test the same known-positive and known-negative samples on both the old and new kit side by side for at least one full run. This catches calibration or sensitivity drift before it reaches a patient report, and it's standard NABL-lab practice regardless of which supplier you're evaluating.

## Ordering for a busy lab

- **Standing order vs per-batch ordering** — labs running consistent monthly volumes get better pricing and priority restocking on a standing order versus ad-hoc purchases.
- **Expiry-aware stocking** — ELISA kits have finite shelf life; order to match your actual monthly test volume rather than over-stocking for a "bulk discount" that expires unused.
- **Backorder handling** — confirm what happens if a specific kit SKU is temporarily out — whether the supplier holds your order date or you lose your place in the queue.
