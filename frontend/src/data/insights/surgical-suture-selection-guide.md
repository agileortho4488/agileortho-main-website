## The four workhorses

**Polyglactin 910 (braided, absorbable).** The general-purpose absorbable — subcutaneous closure, muscle, bowel anastomosis support. Holds strength ~3 weeks, absorbs by ~70 days. The highest-volume suture in most hospitals.

**Polyamide / Nylon (monofilament, non-absorbable).** Skin closure and retention. Minimal tissue reaction, slides clean, needs 5–6 secure throws. Black monofilament on a reverse-cutting needle is the standard skin set.

**Silk (braided, non-absorbable).** Best handling and knot security of any material — ligatures, securing drains, mucosal work. Trade-off: highest tissue reaction; avoided where long-term implant behaviour matters.

**Polypropylene (monofilament, non-absorbable).** The vascular and hernia workhorse — permanent, inert, zero capillarity. Cardiovascular anastomosis, mesh fixation, tendon repair.

Plus the specialists: **catgut** (still used in some obstetric and pediatric settings), **polyester** (valve and tendon work needing permanent braided strength), and **barbed sutures** (knotless closure in laparoscopic and orthopedic wound closure).

## Matching material to closure — quick table

| Closure | First choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Skin (OPD/minor) | Polyamide 3-0/4-0, cutting needle | Low reaction, easy removal |
| Subcutaneous | Polyglactin 3-0/4-0 | Absorbs, no removal visit |
| Fascia/abdominal wall | Loop polyglactin 1 or polypropylene 1 | Strength through healing period |
| Vascular | Polypropylene 5-0 to 7-0, taper needle | Permanent, inert, monofilament |
| Ligatures | Silk 2-0/3-0 ties | Knot security, handling |
| Tendon | Polyester or polypropylene | Permanent strength |

## How stores should stock (and stop expiring stock)

- **Follow the 80/20:** roughly 80% of consumption is polyglactin 2-0/3-0/4-0, polyamide 3-0/4-0 and silk ties. Keep depth there; keep breadth thin elsewhere.
- **Needle before material:** most "wrong suture" complaints are wrong needle (curve, cutting vs taper, length). Order by full specification, not just "3-0 vicryl-type."
- **Check foil dates on delivery**, and rotate — sutures expire quietly and write-offs land on the store's budget.
- **Backorders beat over-stocking:** with a supplier that delivers in 24–48h across Telangana, holding 3 months of slow movers is wasted working capital.
